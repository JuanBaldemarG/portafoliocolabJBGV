The Harvard Art Museums launched a new exhibition designed to help visitors understand how conservation, restoration, and digital imaging contribute to the study of historical works. Curators explained that the show combines paintings, sketches, laboratory photographs, and interactive displays so the public can see what lies beneath the visible surface of a piece. Infrared scans, pigment analysis, and archival records reveal how artists revised compositions, how materials changed over time, and how museum experts decide which treatments are appropriate.

Museum educators said the exhibition was planned to serve both casual visitors and students in art history, chemistry, and design. The galleries include side-by-side comparisons of original works and diagnostic images, along with short descriptions that explain how light exposure, humidity, and handling can alter delicate objects. Some displays focus on cases in which conservation uncovered hidden details, while others explore situations where experts intentionally chose minimal intervention to preserve evidence of age and use.

Organizers believe the exhibition can broaden public appreciation for the scientific and ethical decisions involved in museum work. They also hope the program will encourage interdisciplinary teaching by showing that art analysis often depends on collaboration among historians, scientists, photographers, and conservators. The museum plans to accompany the exhibition with talks, workshops, and online materials for visitors who want to continue exploring the topic after their visit.
